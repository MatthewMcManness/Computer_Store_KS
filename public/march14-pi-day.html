🥧 Happy Pi Day — March 14

Today is Pi Day. And π = 3.14159... is hiding inside every computer ever built.

March 14th. 3/14. Get it? Every year on this day, mathematicians, engineers, and tech lovers celebrate the most famous irrational number in existence. Pi is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter. It never ends, never repeats, and appears absolutely everywhere — including in the guts of the machine you're using right now.

Also: March 14, 1879, was the birthday of Albert Einstein. The man who rewrote our understanding of space, time, and gravity was born on Pi Day. The universe has a sense of humor.

🎂Where Pi Day Came From

The first official Pi Day celebration happened on March 14, 1988, at the San Francisco Exploratorium — a hands-on science museum. A physicist named Larry Shaw organized it. Staff marched around in circles and ate fruit pies. It was delightfully nerdy.

In 2009, the U.S. House of Representatives officially designated March 14 as National Pi Day. The resolution passed 391 to 10. (Those 10 votes against will never be forgiven by mathematicians.)

💻Pi Is In Your Computer Right Now

This isn't abstract math trivia. Pi is baked into the hardware and software that powers modern computing in ways most people never think about. Every time your device does something round — renders a circle, calculates a signal, processes audio — it's reaching for Pi.

📡 GPS accuracy — Satellite triangulation uses spherical geometry. Without Pi calculated to many decimal places, your maps app would put you three blocks off.
🎵 Audio processing — MP3s, streaming music, Bluetooth audio — all use Fourier transforms, which are built on Pi. Every song you've ever played through a computer passed through π.
📺 Video & graphics — Round objects in games and video, anti-aliasing on curves, circular UI elements — all calculated using Pi. The circle on your screen is Pi made visible.
🔐 Encryption — The security that protects your passwords, banking, and messages relies on prime number mathematics that Pi underlies. Your data is safe partly because of a Greek letter.
📶 Wi-Fi & 5G signals — Radio waves are sinusoidal — they travel in the shape of π. Your wireless connection literally rides on Pi through the air to reach your device.

🧠The Computers That Chase Pi

Computing Pi to more and more decimal places has been a benchmark for computing power since the very beginning. In 1949, ENIAC — one of the first electronic computers — calculated 2,037 digits of Pi in 70 hours. It was a world record at the time and a genuine test of the machine's limits.

In 2024, a team calculated Pi to 105 trillion decimal places — a feat that took 75 days of continuous computation. They needed 1.5 petabytes of storage just to hold the result. That's 1.5 million gigabytes. For a number.

Every major leap in computing power has been accompanied by a new Pi record. In a very real sense, Pi has been a measuring stick for the entire history of computing. The faster we can compute Pi, the better our machines have gotten. Pi isn't just a math problem. It's a benchmark.

"Calculating Pi is the closest thing mathematicians have to a universal stress test for a computer."

— Computing lore, paraphrased from generations of benchmarkers
🎂Happy Birthday, Einstein

Albert Einstein was born on March 14, 1879 — Pi Day, 146 years ago today. Einstein's general theory of relativity, which describes how mass warps spacetime, relies on tensor calculus that includes π at a fundamental level. The GPS satellites that beam your location down to your phone include a tiny relativistic correction based on Einstein's equations. Every time you get directions, Einstein's math — and Pi — are both quietly at work.

He was also famously bad at remembering phone numbers. He reportedly said there was no point memorizing information you could look up. The man who rewrote physics preferred to keep his brain clear for ideas. We think he'd have appreciated a good search engine.
