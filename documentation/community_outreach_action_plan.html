Community Outreach Action Plan

Pop-Up Repair Kiosk Launch — Step-by-Step Execution Guide
The Computer Store — Topeka, KS
Based on Partner Research • March 2026

Plan at a Glance

This action plan translates the Community Outreach Partner Research (100+ identified venues) into a week-by-week execution roadmap. Every step includes what to prepare before you start, exactly who to contact, what to say, and how to know you succeeded.

The plan is structured in 5 phases over 12 months, starting with a 2-week preparation sprint before any outreach begins. Total upfront investment: ~$275–$375. Expected break-even: Month 1–2.

Recommended Strategy: Hybrid Model

Based on the Cost/Benefit Analysis, this plan follows a Monthly Pop-Up Locations model as the primary revenue engine (2.2x more profitable, 31% lower costs), with premium house calls offered as an upsell for customers who can't wait for the next scheduled pop-up day ($25–$50 surcharge). All customer calls go to the main shop number. Jobs are tracked in RepairShopr like any other ticket.

Pre-Launch Preparation

Weeks −2 to 0 • Complete ALL items before making any outreach calls
Blocker: Do not skip this phase. Every item below must be complete before you pick up the phone. First impressions with community partners are permanent. Showing up unprepared — without a flyer, without a clear pitch, without a booking URL — kills the opportunity. You only get one shot with each partner.
P1
Design & Print Marketing Materials
Days 1–5 $125–$175 Blocker

Create all print materials. These will be used during partner pitches, left at venues, and distributed to customers.

What to Prepare First
  • Finalize service menu with pricing for on-site and take-back services
  • Confirm the 10% senior discount policy
  • Phone number decided: use main shop number for all outreach. Add a dedicated outreach line later only if call volume becomes unmanageable.
  • Have the QR code ready (already generated: qr-code-computerstoreks-poster.png)
  • Decide on "The Computer Store — We Come to You" as the tagline (or choose another)
Materials to Create
  • Service Flyer (8.5×11, full color, double-sided) — Side A: headline, QR code, "We Come to You" messaging, top 6 services with prices. Side B: full service menu, senior discount callout, how to book. Print 500 copies.
  • Partner One-Sheet (8.5×11, full color) — Tailored pitch for venue owners. "What's In It For You" section. Three partnership models (senior center, small business, library). No-risk/no-cost emphasis. Print 50 copies.
  • Tabletop Sign (5×7 tent card) — "Free Tech Help Here Every [Day]" with QR code. For use at partner venues. Print 20 cards.
  • Business Cards — Already have business cards on hand. Reorder with updated outreach info when current stock runs low.
  • Poster (11×17) — For community bulletin boards, churches, post offices. Large QR code. "Computer Problems? We Come to [Town Name]." Print 30 posters (leave town name blank to hand-write or have town-specific batches).
  • Vehicle Magnet Signs (12×24) — Logo, phone number, "Mobile Computer Repair" and website. Order 2.
Where to Print
  • Flyers, one-sheets, posters: Staples, Office Depot, or local Topeka print shop (compare pricing)
  • Business cards: Vistaprint or MOO (online, cheaper for bulk)
  • Vehicle magnets: Signs.com or Vistaprint (5–7 day lead time)
  • Tabletop tent cards: Staples or print on cardstock and fold
Done When
  • All materials printed and organized in a folder/box
  • Partner one-sheets reviewed by someone other than creator for clarity
  • QR code on every single piece scans correctly to ComputerStoreKS.com

P3
Website Updates — Location Booking & Outreach Page
Days 3–8 $0 Blocker

Customers who scan the QR code need to land on a page that explains the outreach program and lets them book an appointment. This must be live before flyers go out.

What to Prepare First
  • Build or find a scheduling system that coordinates multi-location routing (needs to handle different towns on different days, appointment slots per location, and driving logistics). Evaluate: custom Supabase solution, Acuity Scheduling, or a route-aware booking tool. Simple Calendly won't cut it for multi-location coordination.
  • Integrate with RepairShopr for ticket tracking — all outreach jobs should create tickets in RepairShopr just like shop work (we already use it for inventory, invoicing, and ticket management)
  • Write service descriptions for the outreach page (reuse from plan document)
Website Changes Needed
  • New page: /services/mobile-repair — "We Come to You" page explaining the pop-up kiosk program, service menu with prices, senior discount mention, FAQ, and booking link. Show "coming soon" placeholders for towns until partnerships are confirmed.
  • Update /services hub page — Add "Mobile Repair" as a new service card linking to the new page
  • Booking form/widget — Fields: name, phone, email, town/location preference, device type, brief description of issue. Confirmation email via Resend. All bookings should also create a ticket in RepairShopr.
  • QR code landing — Ensure the QR code on flyers directs to the right page (or to homepage if general)
Important: Homepage & schedule updates come AFTER partnerships are confirmed. Do not add specific town names, locations, or schedules to the homepage or mobile repair page until you have a signed/confirmed partnership. Adding "Now serving Holton!" before you actually have a Holton venue locked down looks unprofessional if someone checks and you're not there yet. Use "coming soon" placeholders until then.
Done When
  • Mobile repair page is live at computerstoreks.com/services/mobile-repair (with "coming soon" placeholders for unconfirmed towns)
  • Multi-location scheduler is functional (or at minimum, a booking form that lets customers select a town)
  • Bookings create tickets in RepairShopr automatically
  • QR code scan on phone lands on the correct page
  • Page looks good on mobile (most users will come from QR scan on phone)
Post-Partnership Updates (do these AFTER Phase 1 partnerships are confirmed):
  • Add confirmed town names, partner locations, and schedule to the mobile repair page
  • Add a banner or card to the homepage: "Now serving [Town Names]! Book a visit."
  • Remove "coming soon" placeholders as towns are confirmed
P4
Create Business Forms & Templates
Days 3–7 $0
Documents to Create
  • Customer Intake Form (paper) — Name, phone, email, device type, serial number, issue description, authorization to work on device, signature line. Include liability waiver ("not responsible for data loss" standard language). Print 50 copies.
  • Partnership Agreement (1-page) — Simple, friendly. Covers: what CSK provides (tech, signage, marketing), what partner provides (table/space, promotion to members), commission structure if applicable (5–10% or free IT for partner), schedule, 30-day cancellation by either party. NOT a binding contract — a handshake formalized. Have 10 copies ready.
  • Take-Back Receipt — For devices that need shop work. Device description, customer info, estimated turnaround, estimated cost range, pickup location preference (shop or next visit). Carbonless duplicate: one copy for customer, one for shop. Print 50.
  • Outreach Tracking via RepairShopr — We already use RepairShopr for ticket tracking, inventory, and invoicing. Tag all outreach tickets with a "Mobile Outreach" tag or custom field so they can be filtered. Supplement with a simple spreadsheet (date, location, customers seen, notes) for per-location metrics that RepairShopr doesn't natively track.
  • Partner Outreach Log — Track all outreach attempts: organization name, contact person, phone, email, date called, outcome, follow-up date, notes, status (prospect/contacted/interested/confirmed/declined).
Done When
  • All forms printed and in the mobile kit
  • Partnership agreement reviewed (consider having a lawyer glance at it, or use a standard template)
  • Tracking spreadsheet set up and accessible on mobile
P5
Verify Insurance & Legal Coverage
Days 1–5 $0–$50
Actions
  • Call your business insurance provider. Ask: "Does my general liability policy cover me performing computer repair services at third-party locations (libraries, senior centers, coffee shops)?" If not, ask about a rider/endorsement.
  • Verify vehicle insurance covers business use (commuting to work sites)
  • Confirm you have a valid Topeka business license (and check if any target counties require separate licensing)
  • Ensure intake forms include adequate liability language (not responsible for pre-existing hardware failure, data loss disclaimer)
Important: Some partners (especially libraries and senior centers) may ask "Do you have insurance?" during the pitch. Being able to immediately say "Yes, we carry general liability" builds instant credibility. Have your policy number handy.
P6
Write Phone Scripts & Email Templates
Days 5–8 $0

Write these out word-for-word. Practice them. The goal is to sound natural, not read from a script — but having the script prevents fumbling on the first call.

Phone Script — Senior Center / Library Director
Opening (15 seconds) "Hi, this is [Name] from The Computer Store in Topeka. I'm reaching out because we're starting a free program to bring computer repair and tech help directly to communities like yours, so folks don't have to drive all the way into Topeka. Do you have just two minutes?"
Pitch (30 seconds) "Here's the idea: we'd send a professional technician to your [center/library] on a regular schedule — say, every Tuesday afternoon. Your [members/patrons] could walk in with their laptop, phone, or tablet and get help on the spot. Things like virus removal, slow computer tune-ups, setting up email, transferring photos — the stuff people struggle with. We offer a 10% senior discount on everything. There's zero cost or risk to you — all we need is a table and two chairs."
Close "Would it make sense for me to stop by for 10 minutes to show you what we do? I could bring a flyer your members could look at. What day works best for you this week?"
Phone Script — Small Business Owner (Coffee Shop, Barbershop)
Opening "Hi, is this [Owner Name]? This is [Name] from The Computer Store in Topeka. I love what you've built at [business name] — I actually [have been there / heard great things]. Quick question: would you be open to a partnership that brings extra foot traffic to your shop at zero cost?"
Pitch "We're bringing mobile computer repair to [town name]. The idea is simple: one day a month, we set up a small repair station at a local business. Your customers see a convenient new service, and our customers buy coffee while they wait. We can either pay you a small commission on services we perform, or we'll handle all your business IT needs for free — your call. Either way, there's no lease, no commitment, and we can try it once to see if it works."

Run First Pop-Up Events
Weeks 3–5 Revenue Starts
Before Each Pop-Up Day
  • Confirm with partner venue 2 days before ("Just confirming I'll see you Tuesday at 10am!")
  • Charge all devices (laptop, hotspot, phone)
  • Load mobile kit in vehicle the night before
  • Check weather — if severe, reschedule and notify partner + any booked customers
  • Review any pre-booked appointments for that location
  • Bring cash for making change (or go card-only with Square)
Day-Of Execution
  • Arrive 15 minutes early to set up
  • Set up table, signage, tabletop tent card, flyer stack, business cards
  • Greet everyone warmly — even if they don't need service today, give them a card
  • For each customer: fill out intake form, diagnose, fix on-site or create take-back receipt
  • Offer Silver Plan to every customer (upsell opportunity)
  • Collect customer email for follow-up (ask permission)
  • Before leaving: thank the partner venue contact by name, leave extra flyers, confirm next visit date
After Each Pop-Up Day
  • All service tickets should already be in RepairShopr (tagged "Mobile Outreach"). Log per-location summary data: location, customers seen, walk-ins vs appointments, Silver Plan signups, take-backs, partner feedback notes.
  • Send follow-up text/email to any customers with take-back devices ("Your laptop is at our Topeka shop, we'll have an update by Friday")
  • Note any feedback from the partner or observations (busy time vs. slow time, better location in venue, etc.)
Phase 1 Success Criteria
  • 2–3 locations running on a weekly schedule
  • Average 4–6 customers per pop-up day
  • Average revenue $200–$400 per pop-up day
  • At least 1 Silver Plan signup per week
  • Partner satisfaction: "Yes, we want to continue"

Phase 1 Milestone: Proof of Concept

  • 2–3 confirmed partner locations running monthly
  • First month revenue tracked and positive
  • Customer feedback collected
  • Partner feedback collected
  • Route schedule published on website (only confirmed locations)
  • Homepage updated with confirmed partner towns
  • At least 1 take-back device returned at next visit
  • RepairShopr ticket tagging working for outreach jobs
Premium House Calls: Once pop-ups are running, customers who call the shop with urgent issues between scheduled pop-up days can be offered a premium house call ($25–$50 surcharge on top of service price). This is not the primary model — it's an upsell for customers who can't wait. Route these through the main shop number and schedule them on non-pop-up days.

Quarterly Performance Review
Month 6, then every 3 months
Review Process
  • Export full tracking spreadsheet data
  • Calculate per-location metrics: avg revenue/visit, avg customers/visit, Silver Plan conversion rate, take-back rate
  • Rank all locations by profitability (revenue minus drive time/fuel cost)

  • Calculate total program P&L: revenue - (tech wages + fuel + materials + commissions + ads)
