⌚ 10 Years Ago Today

On March 9, 2015, Apple held its "Spring Forward" event and changed wearable technology forever.

You probably know the Apple Watch. You may even be wearing one right now. But the story of how it came to exist — and what it meant for the world of tech — is one worth telling.

🤔Where Did the Idea Come From?

It started with a problem Apple had been watching for years. Smartphones were becoming indispensable — but people were becoming slaves to them, pulling them out of pockets hundreds of times a day, staring at screens in the middle of conversations, at dinner tables, on dates.

Apple's answer wasn't to make people use their phones less. It was to put the most important information right on your wrist, so you didn't have to reach for your phone at all.

"There was a sense that technology was going to move onto the body. We felt like the natural place, the place that had historical relevance and significance, was the wrist."

— Alan Dye, Apple Human Interface Group

Development began in secret around 2012. Hundreds of engineers and designers worked for nearly three years, building dozens of functioning prototypes. When Tim Cook finally unveiled it at the iPhone 6 event in September 2014, the tech world erupted.

But it was March 9, 2015 — the "Spring Forward" event — where Apple confirmed the release date, announced full pricing, and made it real. Six weeks later, on April 24, 2015, the Apple Watch went on sale and immediately became the best-selling smartwatch in history.

The watch that put a computer on your wrist.

⏱️A Decade of the Watch
2015First Apple Watch launches in three editions: Watch, Sport, and solid gold Edition. First major new Apple product category since the iPad in 2010.
2016Series 2 arrives with built-in GPS and water resistance — Apple drops the gold Edition entirely after realizing no one needs a $17,000 watch.
2017Series 3 adds cellular connectivity — for the first time, the Watch can work without an iPhone nearby at all.
2018Series 4 gets an ECG sensor — an FDA-cleared electrocardiogram on your wrist. The Apple Watch begins its pivot from gadget to health device.
2020Blood oxygen monitoring added. During the pandemic, Apple Watch becomes a genuine health monitoring tool for millions.
2022Apple Watch Ultra launches — a rugged, titanium version built for extreme athletes, with 60-hour battery life and a siren for emergencies.
2024Series 10 marks a decade of Apple Watch. Sleep apnea detection added — an FDA-cleared feature that can alert users to a serious medical condition they may not know they have.

"Apple Watch begins a new chapter in the way we relate to technology."

— Tim Cook, CEO of Apple, March 9, 2015
🏆Why It Actually Mattered

The Apple Watch wasn't the first smartwatch. Pebble launched in 2013. Samsung had the Galaxy Gear. But Apple did what Apple always does — it didn't invent the category, it defined it.

Within a year, Apple Watch outsold the entire Swiss watch industry combined. It set the template every smartwatch maker still follows today. And perhaps more importantly, it opened a new frontier: health tech on the body.

There are now documented cases of Apple Watch saving lives — detecting irregular heart rhythms, alerting users to falls, catching atrial fibrillation before a stroke. A watch. Saving lives. Ten years after Tim Cook strapped one on his wrist on a stage in San Francisco.
