🌐 March 16, 1995 — On This Day in Tech History

On March 16, 1995, Ward Cunningham launched a website where anyone could edit any page. No ads. No algorithm. No agenda. Just trust.

🌐The Idea That Changed Everything

On March 16, 1995, a programmer named Ward Cunningham quietly launched a website with a peculiar name: WikiWikiWeb. A website where anyone — literally anyone — could edit any page. No password. No approval. No editor. Just pure, open, collaborative trust.

Cunningham named it after the "Wiki Wiki" shuttle buses at Honolulu Airport — wiki wiki meaning "quick" in Hawaiian. He wanted something fast and simple. What he got was the foundation of a new kind of internet.

"The simplest online database that could possibly work."

— Ward Cunningham, describing WikiWikiWeb, 1995

What made Cunningham's invention radical wasn't the technology — it was the philosophy. He believed most people, given the chance, would contribute honestly. That the community would self-correct. That openness was a feature, not a vulnerability. In 1995, that was a genuinely wild bet.

📅From One Page to Everything
1995
Ward Cunningham launches WikiWikiWeb — the world's first wiki — as a tool for software developers to share ideas.
2001
Jimmy Wales and Larry Sanger launch Wikipedia, directly inspired by Cunningham's model. It starts with a single article.
2003
Wikipedia surpasses 100,000 articles. Britannica editors call it a fad. They were wrong.
2006
Time magazine names "You" — the collaborative internet user — Person of the Year. Wikipedia is Exhibit A.
2024
Wikipedia serves over 15 billion page views per month. Non-profit. Ad-free. Still running on trust.

"Wiki is a celebration of the freedom to create and connect ideas."

— Ward Cunningham

Today, wiki technology powers everything from massive knowledge bases to internal company docs. Every time you look something up on Wikipedia, you're using the direct descendant of what one programmer launched on a quiet Tuesday in 1995. Not bad for something named after a shuttle bus.
