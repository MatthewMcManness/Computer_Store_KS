This analysis compares two service delivery models for The Computer Store's rural community outreach program targeting 12 towns within 45 minutes of Topeka, KS:


Model A — Appointment Only: A technician drives to individual customer homes or businesses on demand. No fixed schedule, no partner locations. Customers call and book; the tech drives out for one-off visits.


Model B — One Day a Month at Specific Locations: The tech sets up at a partner venue (library, senior center, coffee shop) on a fixed monthly schedule. Customers book 30–60 minute slots in advance. Walk-ins welcome if time allows.


Both models use an existing employee (no new hire required). The key question is which model generates more revenue per hour of tech time invested, while building sustainable community relationships.

2. Model Definitions

Model A: Appointment Only

On-Demand House Calls

How It Works

  • Customer calls/books online
  • Tech drives to their home or business
  • One customer per trip (occasionally two if nearby)
  • No fixed schedule — demand-driven
  • Service area: 12 towns within 45 min of Topeka

Scheduling

  • 8–10 appointments per week max
  • 60–90 min per appointment (avg)
  • 30–70 min drive time per appointment (round trip)
  • 2–3 appointments per day realistic

Advantages

  • No partner relationships needed
  • Flexible scheduling
  • Lower marketing costs
  • No commissions or stipends
  • Premium pricing possible ($20–$30 house call fee)

Disadvantages

  • High per-customer travel cost
  • Unpredictable volume
  • No walk-in revenue
  • No community visibility
  • Wasted drive time between jobs
  • Hard to build repeat base

Model B: Monthly Pop-Up Locations

Scheduled Community Kiosks

How It Works

  • Set up at a partner venue on a fixed day each month
  • Customers pre-book 30–60 min slots
  • Walk-ins welcome when slots available
  • One drive per location, multiple customers
  • 4–8 locations across 12 target towns

Scheduling

  • 1 day/month per location (e.g., "1st Tuesday = Holton")
  • 6–8 appointment slots per day
  • 9:00 AM – 4:00 PM at each location
  • 4–8 outreach days per month total

Advantages

  • Batch customers (lower travel cost per job)
  • Walk-in revenue on top of appointments
  • Community presence / brand building
  • Predictable schedule & volume
  • Partner referrals & word-of-mouth
  • Stipend income from senior centers

Disadvantages

  • Requires partner relationships
  • Marketing materials needed
  • Possible commissions (5–10%)
  • Fixed schedule may miss urgent needs
  • Setup/teardown time per visit
  • Time to build reputation per town

12. Recommendation

Model B: Monthly Pop-Up Locations is the Clear Winner

Model B generates 2.2x more profit in Year 1 ($52,700 vs $23,700), costs 31% less per month to operate, drives 60% fewer miles, and builds a defensible competitive position in 12 underserved communities. Its worst-case scenario still profits; Model A's worst case loses money. The intangible benefits of community presence, trust-building, and partner referrals create a flywheel that the appointment-only model simply cannot match.

Recommended Approach: Start with Model B, Add Model A as a Premium Upsell

Phase 1 (Months 1–3): Launch Model B

  • Establish 4 pop-up locations (Holton, Perry, Oskaloosa, Silver Lake)
  • One day per month at each location
  • Build partner relationships and community presence
  • Invest $300–$400 upfront in marketing materials

Phase 2 (Months 4–6): Expand Model B + Test Model A

  • Add 4 more locations (Rossville, St. Marys, Meriden, Carbondale)
  • Offer "Priority House Calls" as a premium add-on ($25–$50 surcharge)
  • House calls only for customers who can't wait for next pop-up date
  • Evaluate if appointment-only fills gaps between pop-up days

Phase 3 (Months 7–12): Optimize & Scale

  • 8–12 pop-up locations running monthly
  • High-demand locations move to biweekly or weekly visits
  • Consider second technician when net profit exceeds $4,000/mo consistently
  • House calls remain a premium option, not the primary model

The hybrid approach captures the best of both worlds: Model B's efficiency and community-building as the core revenue engine, with Model A's house calls available as a premium service for customers willing to pay extra for convenience.

Decision Triggers: When to Reassess

Trigger Action

3+ locations requesting additional days Move top locations from monthly to biweekly
