Executive Summary

The Big Idea

Small towns surrounding Topeka are drastically underserved for computer repair. Residents either drive 30-60 minutes to Topeka or Best Buy in Lawrence, or they don't get help at all. Many of these people are seniors who struggle with the trip and just want someone local they can trust.

We partner with small businesses, senior centers, and community hubs in these towns to set up appointment-based satellite stations — essentially pop-up kiosks where a CSK technician comes on scheduled days to meet with local customers. We fix small issues on-site, and bring larger repairs back to the Topeka shop.

The cost to us is near zero. We don't lease space. We negotiate commission-sharing with business partners (or get paid a stipend by senior centers). A single tech with a laptop bag, toolkit, and the company vehicle can cover 3-4 towns per week, building a customer base in communities that have no local computer repair option.

How It Works

A low-overhead model that saturates rural markets through strategic community partnerships

1 Partner with Local Venues

Identify small businesses, senior centers, libraries, and community centers in small towns. Negotiate a small space (a table and two chairs is all we need) for scheduled appointment hours.

  • Coffee shops, insurance offices, real estate agencies
  • Senior centers and community buildings
  • Libraries and churches with community rooms
  • Small businesses that want foot traffic

2 Schedule Appointment Days

Each partner location gets a recurring half-day or full-day slot. Customers book appointments through our website or by calling the shop. Walk-ins welcome if time allows.

  • Monday: Perry / Oskaloosa
  • Tuesday: Holton (already established)
  • Wednesday: Silver Lake / Rossville
  • Thursday: Carbondale / Overbrook
  • Friday: Flex day / overflow / new markets

3 On-Site Quick Fixes

The tech handles quick jobs on the spot: virus removal, tune-ups, data transfers from a portable drive, software installs, consultations, and device setup.

  • Most common issues resolved in 30-60 minutes
  • Portable toolkit + laptop for diagnostics
  • Portable external drive for quick data work
  • Hotspot or partner WiFi for cloud services

4 Bring Back Big Jobs

Hardware repairs, motherboard replacements, data recovery, and custom builds go back to the Topeka shop. The tech picks them up one visit and returns them the next.

  • Tag and receipt for every machine taken
  • Customer gets status updates via text/email
  • Returned on next scheduled visit (or sooner)
  • Delivery option for immobile/elderly customers

5 Upsell & Recurring Revenue

Every interaction is a chance to enroll customers in Silver Plan protection, sell refurbished machines, or sign up small businesses for MSP services.

  • Silver Plan enrollments (recurring monthly)
  • Refurbished PC sales with delivery
  • MSP contracts for local small businesses
  • Antivirus and backup solution sales

6 Saturate the Market

With near-zero overhead per location, we can cover every small town within 45 minutes of Topeka. Competitors can't justify opening a shop in these towns — but we don't need a shop.

  • No lease, no utilities, no build-out
  • Scale by adding days, not locations
  • Brand presence in 8-12 communities
  • Word-of-mouth in tight-knit towns spreads fast

Partnership Models

Three partnership structures designed so everyone wins

Senior Centers & Community Orgs

They pay us — we provide a needed community service

How It Works

  • Senior center contracts CSK for scheduled tech days
  • They pay a flat stipend per visit ($50-150/day)
  • Members get discounted rates on services
  • We handle sign-ups, they promote to members

Why They Want This

  • Tech help is the #1 requested service at senior centers
  • Increases membership value and attendance
  • Grant funding often available for tech literacy programs
  • Positions them as a community resource hub
Revenue Model:

Stipend from center + full service fees from customers. Potentially pitch as a grant-funded "Digital Literacy" partner.

Small Business Partners

Commission split — we bring them foot traffic

How It Works

  • Coffee shop, barbershop, or office lends us a corner/table
  • We pay small commission on repairs done at their location
  • OR they get free tech support for their own business
  • Co-branded signage and flyers at the location

Ideal Business Partners

  • Coffee shops (customers wait while we work)
  • Insurance/real estate offices (spare conference room)
  • Barbershops and salons (community hubs)
  • Hardware stores and farm supply (trusted local spots)
Revenue Model:

5-10% commission on services performed at location, OR free IT support for the partner's business (often more valuable to them).

Libraries & Public Spaces

Free space in exchange for community tech support

How It Works

  • Library provides a meeting room on scheduled days
  • No cost to us — positioned as community service
  • We help patrons with their personal devices
  • Library promotes through their newsletter and bulletin board

Why Libraries Love This

  • Patrons constantly ask staff for tech help
  • Aligns with digital equity and literacy missions
  • Drives foot traffic and program attendance numbers
  • No cost to the library
Revenue Model:

No commission owed. Free space + free promotion. All service revenue is ours. Potential for library to fund via programming budget.

The Partnership Pitch

For every partner type, the pitch is simple: "We'll bring a professional computer technician to your location on a regular schedule. Your customers/members/patrons get convenient tech help without driving to Topeka. You get foot traffic, happy members, or a small commission. There's no cost or risk to you."

Daily Operations

A day in the life of the outreach technician

8:00 AM
Load up at the shop. Check schedule, grab any repaired machines for return delivery, pack toolkit and portable supplies.
8:30 AM
Drive to first town. Most locations are 15-35 minutes from the shop. Use drive time for customer call-backs.
9:00 AM
Set up at partner location. Table, signage, toolkit. Quick hello to the business owner/staff. Return any machines from last visit.
9:15 AM
Appointments begin. 30-60 min slots. Quick fixes handled on the spot. Larger jobs tagged and collected for the shop.
12:00 PM
Lunch / drive to second location. If covering two towns, drive to the afternoon location. Eat local — builds relationships.
12:30 PM
Afternoon appointments. Same format. Senior centers often prefer afternoon slots.
3:30 PM
Pack up and head back. Drop collected machines at the Topeka shop. Brief the team on incoming jobs.
4:00 PM
Back at shop. Log repairs, update tickets, confirm next day's schedule. Handle any quick follow-ups.

Kit Tech's Mobile Kit

  • Laptop with diagnostic tools and remote access software
  • Portable SSD for data transfers and backups
  • USB toolkit (bootable drives, drivers, AV tools)
  • Basic hardware toolkit (screwdrivers, pry tools, thermal paste)
  • Mobile hotspot (for locations without reliable WiFi)
  • Receipt printer or tablet for invoicing on-site
  • CSK branded tablecloth, signage, and business cards
  • Intake forms and device tags for take-back repairs

Flow Customer Flow

  • Book: Customer calls shop or books online, selects their town
  • Arrive: Customer brings device to the partner location at their time slot
  • Diagnose: Tech assesses the issue, provides quote
  • Fix or Tag: Quick fix done on-site, or machine tagged for shop repair
  • Pay: Payment via Square/Stripe on tablet or phone
  • Follow-up: For shop repairs, customer gets updates via text
  • Return: Repaired machines delivered on next scheduled visit

The Real ROI: Customer Lifetime Value

These numbers only show direct service revenue. The real value is in long-term customer relationships. A senior who gets their laptop fixed in Perry becomes a repeat customer for years — tune-ups, new device purchases, Silver Plan subscriptions, and referrals to their neighbors. Implementation Timeline

Start small, prove the model, then expand

Phase 1 — Weeks 1-4

Pilot: 2-3 Towns

Start with the easiest wins: towns closest to Topeka, partners already identified, or where we have existing customer relationships (Holton).

  • Identify and contact 4-6 potential partner venues
  • Secure agreements with 2-3 partners (handshake or simple 1-page agreement)
  • Create signage, flyers, business cards for outreach program
  • Add appointment booking to computerstoreks.com (location selector)
  • Run pilot schedule for 2-3 weeks, track every metric
  • Collect customer feedback and partner feedback
Phase 2 — Months 2-3

Expand: 4-6 Towns

With proof of concept from Phase 1, expand to the next ring of towns. Refine the schedule based on demand patterns.

  • Add 2-3 more partner locations based on Phase 1 learnings
  • Optimize scheduling (drop low-demand slots, double up on hot spots)
  • Launch local Facebook ads targeting each town specifically
  • Post flyers at community bulletin boards, churches, post offices
  • Partner with local newspapers for a small feature/ad
  • Begin tracking Silver Plan conversion rate by location
Phase 3 — Months 4-6

Saturate: 8-12 Towns

Full coverage of the Topeka metro rural ring. Evaluate whether demand justifies a second outreach tech.

  • Fill remaining days with Phase 3 towns
  • Evaluate demand for second technician / second route
  • Consider Saturday slots for high-demand locations
  • Develop referral bonus program (existing customers refer neighbors)
  • Explore grant funding for senior digital literacy partnerships
  • Review financial performance and adjust pricing if needed
Phase 4 — Months 6-12

Optimize & Scale

The program is running. Now optimize for profit and consider expanding the radius or adding a second tech.

  • Drop underperforming locations, double up on winners
  • Hire or assign second outreach tech if demand supports it
  • Expand radius to 60 minutes if Phase 3 towns are saturated
  • Formalize partner agreements for annual renewals
  • Create case studies from success stories for marketing
  • Evaluate dedicated outreach vehicle with CSK branding

Risks & Mitigation

! Low Initial Demand

Risk: Nobody shows up the first few weeks.

Mitigation: This is expected. Small towns take time to trust new services. Start with senior centers where there's a built-in audience. Drop flyers at every door. The first 5 happy customers will do more marketing than we ever could. Budget 4-6 weeks before judging a location.

! Partner Drops Out

Risk: A partner venue decides they don't want us anymore.

Mitigation: Always have 2-3 backup venues identified per town. Our agreements are informal and easy to move. The library or community center is always an option. We should never be dependent on a single venue in any town.

! Employee Vehicle Issues

Risk: Vehicle breaks down or weather prevents travel.

Mitigation: Have a backup plan for rescheduling. Customers at partner locations can be called to reschedule. Weather days are rare enough to handle ad hoc. Long-term: consider a dedicated service vehicle.

! Liability at Partner Locations

Risk: Device gets damaged or lost at a partner location.

Mitigation: Our existing business insurance should cover off-site work — verify with our agent. Use intake forms with liability language. Tag every device with a numbered receipt. Never leave customer devices unattended at partner locations.

! Tech Stretched Too Thin

Risk: One person can't cover enough towns or demand exceeds capacity.

Mitigation: This is a good problem. Start conservative (2-3 towns), only expand when the schedule is consistently full. If we're turning people away, that's the signal to add a second tech. Overflow can be directed to the Topeka shop.

! Competition Copies the Model

Risk: Another repair shop starts doing the same thing.

Mitigation: First-mover advantage is huge in small towns. Once we're the "computer guy" in Perry, it's very hard for a competitor to displace us. Relationships and reputation are the moat. Move fast to lock in partnerships before anyone else thinks of this.

Why This Works for CSK

Zero Barrier to Entry

No leases, no build-outs, no upfront investment beyond signage. If a location doesn't work, we walk away with zero loss. This is the lowest-risk expansion strategy possible.

Unserved Markets

These towns literally have no option. We're not competing with anyone — we're filling a vacuum. The demand exists; nobody's serving it.

Built-In Trust Network

By partnering with businesses and organizations the community already trusts, we inherit that trust. The senior center's endorsement is worth more than any ad campaign.

Compounds Over Time

Every repair builds reputation. Every customer tells their neighbor. Every town we're in makes the next one easier to pitch. This model gets stronger the longer we run it.

Pipeline for Everything Else

Every outreach customer is a lead for Silver Plans, refurbished PC sales, MSP services for their business, and custom builds. This isn't just a repair play — it's a customer acquisition channel.

Defensible Position

Once we're established as the town's tech provider, switching costs are high. Relationships, reputation, and convenience create a moat that a new competitor can't easily cross.

Immediate Next Steps

To Get Started This Month

  1. Pick the first 2-3 towns — Recommend Silver Lake (closest), Perry, and Holton (existing relationship).
  2. Make 6-8 phone calls — Contact senior centers, libraries, and 1-2 small businesses in each pilot town. Use the partnership pitch from this plan.
  3. Secure 2-3 partner agreements — Even a verbal "yes, come set up a table on Tuesdays" is enough to start.
  4. Print materials — Flyers, business cards, a branded tablecloth, and device intake tags. Budget: ~$200.
  5. Assign the outreach tech — Pick the most personable, self-directed tech. This role is part technician, part salesperson, part community ambassador.
  6. Add online booking — Add a location picker to the website booking flow so customers can select their town.
  7. Run the pilot for 4 weeks — Track appointments, revenue, customer satisfaction, and partner feedback.
  8. Review and expand — After 4 weeks, evaluate results and decide which Phase 2 towns to add.
